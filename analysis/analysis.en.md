This section places the measured trees in context: how large they are within and between species, and how they compare with the circumferences that have been proposed or used as criteria for protecting trees as natural monuments. Sizes are trunk circumferences in metres. Where a tree was measured more than once (for example, trees with several trunks), every measurement is included. The measurement method is based on the standard practice of measuring diameter at breast height—that is, the tree's circumference at a height of 1.3 m. In cases where measurement at this height was impossible (e.g., due to early branching), the measurement was taken at the nearest possible height.

For comparison, there are currently 288 living natural monuments in the Tricity (including groups and avenues counted as single units), 124 of which are located within the Tricity Landscape Park alone. Including the trees we have proposed would significantly increase the number of designated monuments (by over 30%).

A threshold is a reference point, not a legal determination: formal recognition as a natural monument is a separate administrative decision. Nevertheless, with the exception of spruces, all average measurement values of our trees meet the criteria proposed in the aforementioned publication. Due to the small number of spruces occurring in the Tricity Landscape Park, we believe that the individuals we measured are suitable for designation as natural monuments, as they represent some of the largest specimens of this genus in the area.
